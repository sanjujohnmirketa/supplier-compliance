# Beacon Health Systems — Supplier Quality &amp; Accreditation Requirements

Document type: Supplier-facing standard (Quality domain)
Issuing entity: Beacon Health Systems (US healthcare delivery &amp; device network — fictional, for demo)
Standard ID: BHS-STD-Q-003
Version: v1.0
Effective from: 2026-01-01
Grounding source: Themes adapted from ISO 13485:2016 (medical device QMS), ISO 9001:2015,
The Joint Commission accreditation standards, and CLIA (Clinical Laboratory Improvement
Amendments) for laboratory-services suppliers. Adapted, not reproduced.
Note: Illustrative demo baselines, not legal advice.

---

## 1. Purpose &amp; Scope

1.1 This standard defines the quality-management evidence a supplier must hold and
maintain to be approved to supply devices, pharmaceutical products, laboratory
services, or clinical services to Beacon Health Systems facilities. It applies to all
device and pharmaceutical suppliers; clinical-service and laboratory suppliers follow
Section 4; indirect and administrative suppliers follow the reduced baseline in
Section 5.

---

## 2. Medical Device Quality Management System

2.1 Suppliers of medical devices or device components must hold a current ISO 13485
certificate. The certificate scope must explicitly cover the products supplied to
Beacon, and must be within its validity window as of the evaluation date.

2.2 The issuing registrar must be accredited under a recognized accreditation body.
Certificates from non-accredited registrars are not accepted.

2.3 Device suppliers must maintain a documented Device Master Record and Design
History File and make them available on request, consistent with 21 CFR Part 820
design-control expectations.

---

## 3. Pharmaceutical &amp; Distribution Quality

3.1 Pharmaceutical manufacturers and distributors must hold current Good
Manufacturing Practice (GMP) certification appropriate to their role in the supply
chain, and must be licensed/registered with the applicable state Board of Pharmacy
and, for manufacturers, registered with the FDA.

3.2 Distributors must maintain chain-of-custody / pedigree documentation for each
lot, consistent with the Drug Supply Chain Security Act (DSCSA).

---

## 4. Clinical &amp; Laboratory Services

4.1 Suppliers of clinical or diagnostic laboratory services must hold current CLIA
certification appropriate to the complexity of testing performed, and current
accreditation from a CMS-approved accreditation organization (for example, CAP or
The Joint Commission) where applicable.

4.2 Suppliers of clinical staffing or contracted clinical services must provide
evidence of current facility accreditation and, where applicable, the credentialing
status of the personnel provided.

---

## 5. Reduced Baseline (Indirect / Administrative)

5.1 Indirect and administrative suppliers must hold ISO 9001 where the service
affects product or service conformity. Purely administrative services may be
approved without ISO 9001 at the reviewer's discretion, recorded with a reason at the
governance gate.

---

## 6. Validity &amp; Renewal

6.1 ISO 13485 operates on a three-year certification cycle with annual surveillance
audits. CLIA certification is renewed on a two-year cycle. A certificate that has
lapsed, been suspended, or fallen outside its surveillance schedule is treated as a
non-compliance pending renewal and blocks advancement past the governance gate.

6.2 A change of manufacturing site, a major nonconformity at audit, an FDA warning
letter, or a customer-specific escalation triggers re-evaluation of the supplier's
quality status.

---

## 7. Audit Trail

7.1 The certificate number, registrar, scope, and validity dates extracted from each
quality certificate are written to the append-only audit trail with a pointer to the
source document and the actor that recorded them.

End of standard.
