# Meridian Drive Systems — Responsible Minerals Sourcing Standard

Document type: Supplier-facing operational standard (Conflict Minerals domain)
Issuing entity: Meridian Drive Systems Inc. (US automotive OEM — fictional, for demo)
Standard ID: MDS-STD-CM-001
Version: v1.0
Effective from: 2026-01-01
Supersedes: none
Grounding source: Themes adapted from OECD Due Diligence Guidance for Responsible
Supply Chains of Minerals, US Dodd-Frank Section 1502, and the Responsible Minerals
Initiative (RMI) frameworks. Adapted, not reproduced.
Note: Thresholds below are illustrative demo baselines, not legal advice. [Unverified]
applicability to a specific supplier must be confirmed per case.

---

## 1. Purpose & Applicability

1.1 This standard defines the responsible-sourcing evidence a supplier must submit
when supplying components that contain, or are manufactured using, tin, tantalum,
tungsten, gold (collectively "3TG") or cobalt. It applies to all production
(direct) suppliers shipping to a US destination and to indirect suppliers where the
supplied item contains these minerals.

1.2 A supplier is in scope if any bill-of-materials line, sub-assembly, or raw
material it supplies contains 3TG or cobalt at any concentration above trace levels,
regardless of tier depth in the supply chain.

---

## 2. Required Documentation

2.1 Conflict Minerals Reporting Template (CMRT)
Suppliers of 3TG-bearing components must submit a current CMRT (RMI template,
latest available revision) completed at the company or product level. The CMRT must
declare all smelters and refiners in the supply chain for each in-scope mineral.

2.2 Cobalt Reporting (EMRT)
Suppliers of cobalt, lithium-ion battery cells, cathode active material, or other
battery raw materials must submit a current Extended Minerals Reporting Template
(EMRT) covering cobalt, and an upstream due-diligence statement describing how the
supplier identifies and manages risk in its cobalt supply chain.

2.3 Upstream Due-Diligence Statement
Raw-material and tier-N suppliers must provide a written due-diligence statement
aligned to the five-step OECD framework: (1) management systems, (2) risk
identification, (3) risk mitigation strategy, (4) independent audit of smelters,
(5) annual reporting.

---

## 3. Smelter & Refiner Verification

3.1 Declared smelter and refiner identifiers (RMI CID numbers) are checked against
the Responsible Minerals Assurance Process (RMAP) conformant smelter list current as
of the evaluation date.

3.2 A supplier declaring one or more non-conformant, undeclared, or "unknown"
smelters for an in-scope mineral is routed to the governance gate for mandatory human
review and is not auto-approved.

3.3 Conflict-affected and high-risk areas (CAHRAs), including the Democratic Republic
of the Congo (DRC) and adjoining countries, raise the supplier to HIGH risk tier and
require the full evidence set in Section 2.

---

## 4. Validity & Re-Evaluation

4.1 CMRT and EMRT declarations must be refreshed at least every twelve months, or
sooner when the supplier changes a smelter, adds an in-scope commodity, or when a
declared smelter loses conformant status.

4.2 A declaration past its twelve-month window is treated as a non-compliance pending
renewal and blocks advancement past the governance gate.

---

## 5. Governance & Audit

5.1 Smelter verification results, the parsed CMRT/EMRT contents, and any human
disambiguation of a non-conformant smelter are written to the append-only audit trail
with a timestamp, the actor, and a pointer to the source declaration file.

End of standard.
