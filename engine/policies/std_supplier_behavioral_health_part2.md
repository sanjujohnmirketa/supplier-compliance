# Supplier Standard: Behavioral Health & SUD Confidentiality (42 CFR Part 2)

## Purpose and Scope

This standard defines the confidentiality and accreditation requirements for a
behavioral-health or substance-use-disorder treatment vendor, or any vendor that
receives SUD patient records on such a facility's behalf. 42 CFR Part 2 imposes a
confidentiality regime STRICTER than standard HIPAA, using a legally distinct consent
instrument — this standard applies IN ADDITION TO, not instead of, the standard
HIPAA/BAA requirement.

## Requirements

1. **Part 2 Program Determination.** A facility or vendor is a "Part 2 program" if it is
   federally assisted and holds itself out as providing, or provides, SUD diagnosis,
   treatment, or referral for treatment.
   *Source: 42 CFR 2.11*

2. **Qualified Service Organization Agreement (QSOA).** A vendor that receives SUD
   patient records to provide a service to the Part 2 program must sign a QSOA — a
   distinct instrument from a standard HIPAA Business Associate Agreement, with its own
   redisclosure prohibition.
   *Source: 42 CFR 2.11, 2.12*

3. **Prohibition on Redisclosure.** SUD patient records disclosed under Part 2 carry a
   redisclosure prohibition that travels with the data — the vendor may not redisclose
   the information further except as Part 2 itself permits, regardless of any other
   consent the patient may have given for other purposes.
   *Source: 42 CFR 2.32*

4. **Patient Consent for Disclosure.** Disclosure of SUD patient identifying information
   generally requires the patient's specific written consent, naming the recipient and
   purpose — broader "treatment, payment, and operations" consent common under HIPAA is
   NOT sufficient under Part 2 for most disclosures.
   *Source: 42 CFR 2.31*

5. **CARF or Joint Commission Behavioral Health Accreditation.** The facility should hold
   current accreditation from CARF International or The Joint Commission's behavioral
   health program, verified independently rather than accepted from self-report.
   *Source: CARF International; The Joint Commission*

6. **Breach/Improper Disclosure Notification.** An improper disclosure of Part 2-protected
   information must be reported and remediated per the vendor's Part 2 breach
   procedures, distinct from (and in addition to) standard HIPAA breach notification.
   *Source: 42 CFR Part 2, Subpart D*

## Renewal and Monitoring Reference

| Item | Typical Cycle |
|---|---|
| QSOA validity | Term of the underlying service agreement; reviewed annually |
| CARF/Joint Commission accreditation | 3-year (CARF) or 3-year (Joint Commission) survey cycle |

## Severity if Non-Compliant

**Critical.** Absence of a QSOA where one is required, or evidence of redisclosure
without Part 2-compliant consent, should block approval — this is a stricter
confidentiality regime than HIPAA and a real regulatory exposure if mishandled.
