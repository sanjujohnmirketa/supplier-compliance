# Supplier Standard: CLIA Certification (Laboratory Vendors)

## Purpose and Scope

This standard defines the minimum federal certification, accreditation, and personnel
requirements a laboratory vendor must meet before onboarding is approved and while the
relationship remains active. It applies to any supplier that creates, performs, or reports
results from testing on human specimens, including reference laboratories, genetic testing
laboratories, and diagnostic testing partners.

## Requirements

1. **CLIA Certificate Required.** Any laboratory vendor performing testing on human
   specimens must hold a valid CLIA certificate issued by CMS through the applicable state
   agency before onboarding can be approved.
   *Source: 42 CFR 493.35*

2. **Certificate Type Must Match Test Complexity.** The vendor's CLIA certificate type
   (Certificate of Waiver, Certificate for Provider-Performed Microscopy Procedures,
   Certificate of Compliance, or Certificate of Accreditation) must correspond to the
   complexity of testing performed. Moderate- and high-complexity testing requires a
   Certificate of Compliance or Certificate of Accreditation.
   *Source: 42 CFR 493.3*

3. **Accreditation Required for Certificate of Accreditation Holders.** Laboratories
   holding a Certificate of Accreditation must be accredited by a CMS-approved
   accreditation organization (for example, CAP, COLA, The Joint Commission, or A2LA).
   *Source: 42 CFR 493.551 et seq.*

4. **Accreditation Scope Must Match the Ordered Test.** The laboratory's accredited scope
   must specifically cover the test type being contracted for. General accreditation does
   not imply coverage of every test on the laboratory's published menu — scope must be
   verified per test, not assumed from the presence of a certificate.
   *Source: CMS-approved accreditor's published checklist (e.g., CAP Accreditation Checklists)*

5. **Proficiency Testing (PT) Enrollment.** The laboratory must enroll in, and
   successfully participate in, a CMS-approved proficiency testing program for each
   regulated analyte it offers.
   *Source: 42 CFR Part 493, Subpart H*

6. **Response to Unsuccessful PT Performance.** An unsuccessful PT event must be
   documented with a root-cause investigation and a corrective action plan. A laboratory
   with unsuccessful performance in two consecutive or two of three consecutive PT events
   for the same analyte is subject to CLIA sanctions, including possible loss of authority
   to test that analyte.
   *Source: 42 CFR 493.801–493.865*

7. **Qualified Laboratory Director.** The laboratory must have a Laboratory Director who
   meets CLIA personnel qualification standards appropriate to the complexity of testing
   performed.
   *Source: 42 CFR 493.1403–493.1495*

8. **Personnel Competency Assessment.** Testing personnel must undergo a documented
   six-element competency assessment at hire, at six months, and annually thereafter.
   *Source: 42 CFR 493.1235 / 493.1451*

9. **Certificate Renewal Cycle.** CLIA certificates are generally valid for two years and
   must be renewed before expiration to maintain testing authorization. Renewal status
   should be re-verified on this cycle, not only at initial onboarding.
   *Source: 42 CFR 493.43*

10. **Certificate Is Not Transferable.** A CLIA certificate does not transfer in the event
    of a change of ownership, laboratory name, or physical location. A new or updated
    certificate must be obtained before testing may continue under the new entity or
    address.
    *Source: 42 CFR 493.43(b)*

## Renewal and Monitoring Reference

| Item | Typical Cycle |
|---|---|
| CLIA Certificate | 2 years |
| Accreditation body inspection (e.g., CAP) | ~2 years |
| Proficiency testing | Ongoing, per scheduled PT event (commonly 3x/year per analyte) |

## Severity if Non-Compliant

**Critical.** A lapsed, mismatched, or absent CLIA certificate, or an accreditation scope
gap for the specific test ordered, should block supplier approval and halt active testing
relationships until resolved.
