# Supplier Standard: USP <797> Sterile Compounding

## Purpose and Scope

This standard defines the facility, personnel, and environmental monitoring requirements
for a vendor compounding sterile preparations — including IV admixtures, injectables, and
ophthalmic preparations — supplied for patient use. Sterile compounding failures carry
direct and immediate patient safety risk, so this standard is treated as Critical severity.

## Requirements

1. **Applicability.** Chapter <797> applies to compounding of all sterile preparations,
   including IV admixtures and injectable medications, whether compounded on-site or
   supplied by an outsourcing facility.
   *Source: USP General Chapter <797>*

2. **Cleanroom / Engineering Controls.** Sterile compounding must occur within a certified
   primary engineering control (for example, a laminar airflow workbench or biological
   safety cabinet) located within an appropriately classified cleanroom or segregated
   compounding area.
   *Source: USP General Chapter <797>*

3. **Personnel Competency for Sterile Compounding.** Personnel must complete garbing
   competency, media-fill testing, and hand hygiene competency before compounding sterile
   preparations, and must requalify on a defined schedule (typically annually, or
   semi-annually for high-risk compounding).
   *Source: USP General Chapter <797>*

4. **Environmental Monitoring.** Viable and non-viable air and surface sampling must be
   performed on a defined schedule to verify the compounding environment continues to meet
   its required ISO air classification.
   *Source: USP General Chapter <797>*

5. **Risk-Level Categorization.** Every compounded sterile preparation (CSP) must be
   categorized by risk level (immediate-use, low, medium, or high), which determines the
   applicable beyond-use date limits and required controls.
   *Source: USP General Chapter <797>*

6. **Beyond-Use Dating for Sterile Preparations.** Beyond-use dates are assigned based on
   CSP risk level, absent sterility testing or stability data supporting an extended date.
   *Source: USP General Chapter <797>*

## Renewal and Monitoring Reference

| Item | Typical Cycle |
|---|---|
| Personnel requalification (garbing, media-fill) | Annual (semi-annual for high-risk compounding) |
| Viable air/surface environmental sampling | Per defined schedule, commonly monthly |
| Certification of engineering controls (PECs, cleanroom) | Every 6 months |

## Severity if Non-Compliant

**Critical.** Sterile compounding deficiencies pose direct and immediate patient safety
risk (contamination, sterility failure) and should be treated as blocking regardless of
the vendor's other compliance standing.
