# Supplier Standard: USP <800> Hazardous Drug Handling

## Purpose and Scope

This standard defines requirements for a vendor that handles hazardous drugs at any stage
— receipt, storage, compounding, administration support, or disposal — to protect
personnel, patients, and the environment from exposure.

## Requirements

1. **Applicability.** Chapter <800> applies to any entity that handles hazardous drugs,
   including receipt, storage, compounding, and disposal, not only compounding activity
   itself.
   *Source: USP General Chapter <800>*

2. **Current Hazardous Drug List Maintained.** The vendor must maintain and reference a
   current hazardous drug list consistent with the NIOSH List of Hazardous Drugs in
   Healthcare Settings.
   *Source: USP General Chapter <800>; NIOSH*

3. **Facility Requirements for Hazardous Drug Handling.** Hazardous drug compounding must
   occur in a containment primary engineering control located within a containment
   secondary engineering control, externally vented where sterile hazardous compounding is
   performed.
   *Source: USP General Chapter <800>*

4. **Personal Protective Equipment (PPE).** Personnel handling hazardous drugs must use
   PPE appropriate to the activity performed (for example, chemotherapy-rated gloves and
   gowns), per a documented PPE policy.
   *Source: USP General Chapter <800>*

5. **Deactivation, Decontamination, and Disposal.** Surfaces and equipment used for
   hazardous drug handling must be deactivated, decontaminated, and cleaned per a
   documented procedure, and hazardous waste disposed of through a permitted hazardous
   waste process.
   *Source: USP General Chapter <800>*

6. **Medical Surveillance.** The vendor should offer medical surveillance to personnel with
   ongoing occupational exposure to hazardous drugs.
   *Source: USP General Chapter <800>*

## Renewal and Monitoring Reference

| Item | Typical Cycle |
|---|---|
| Hazardous drug list review | Annual, or upon NIOSH list revision |
| PPE and containment policy review | Annual |
| Facility engineering control certification | Every 6–12 months |

## Severity if Non-Compliant

**Critical.** Hazardous drug handling deficiencies carry occupational exposure risk to
personnel as well as patient safety risk, and should be treated as blocking.
